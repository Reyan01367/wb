Project 1 — Brand campaign: OLIVARA

The 500-Year Head Start — Hand over a community that already looks established.

Summary

A B2B brand campaign that sells OLIVARA’s centuries-old Spanish olive trees to UAE developers as the fastest way to make a new community feel finished, trusted and liveable.

OLIVARA imports mature Spanish heritage olive trees, then supplies and installs them across the UAE. Its buyers are real estate developers, who usually treat trees as a landscaping line decided late and on cost. The task: move OLIVARA from supplier to sales asset, in a market where 73% of Dubai homes are sold before they are built.

Research highlights

Market

-   215,700 Record Dubai residential transactions in 2025, worth over Dh686.6bn and up 30.9% year on year. (Khaleej Times, 2026a)

-   73% Of Dubai residential sales in 2025 were off-plan: buyers commit to a render, then wait years to move in. (Cavendish Maxwell, 2026)

-   $2.84bn Projected UAE landscaping market by 2030, up from $1.67bn in 2024 (about 9% a year). (Research and Markets, 2025)

Audience

-   40% Higher demand for green, wellness-focused projects, along with price and rental premiums. (Khaleej Times, 2025b)

-   Leading developers already compete on nature: Ghaf Woods (Majid Al Futtaim), The Wilds (Aldar) and Grand Polo (Emaar). (Khaleej Times, 2025b)

-   Buyers pay a premium for biophilic, nature-led environments, and shade makes shared spaces usable for more of the year. (Khaleej Times, n.d.)

Trend

-   3.5m Trees Dubai plans to plant by 2040, alongside 187 km² of green space. (Khaleej Times, 2026b)

-   Dh1m Paid per tree by developers already planting olive trees up to 2,500 years old (MAG, Keturah Reserve). (Khaleej Times, 2025a)

-   2.2°C Cooling from green parks in Abu Dhabi. Olive trees tolerate intense water shortage, so they suit the climate. (Gulf News, 2024; Connor, 2005)

Insight

“Off-plan buyers are asked to believe in a place that doesn’t exist yet. A young tree takes a decade to say “home”; a 500-year-old olive says it on day one.”

Strategy: COM-B diagnosis

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Component                           Barrier
  ----------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Capability                          Developers know green sells, but not that mature trees can be sourced, moved and established in UAE conditions, or what that involves.

  Opportunity                         Landscaping is decided late, by procurement, after the sales launch, so trees never reach the sales and marketing team.

  Motivation (main barrier)           Reflective: trees are seen as a cost line, not a sales asset. Automatic: caution about anything that might not survive on site.
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Creative brief

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Step                                Answer
  ----------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Behaviour                           Specify OLIVARA heritage olives at masterplan stage, and install a “Show Tree” at sales launch.

  Audience                            Marketing directors and masterplanners at UAE developers (Dubai, Abu Dhabi), while launches are being planned.

  Barrier                             “Trees are a landscaping cost for later”, plus doubt that old trees survive the move.

  Trigger                             Reframe the tree as proof: its age becomes a selling point, and its passport becomes reassurance.

  Idea                                The 500-Year Head Start: give a new community a history before it has residents.

  Action                              Book a grove visit or a Show Tree consultation.

  Measurement                         Qualified enquiries, Cityscape meetings booked, Show Trees converted into masterplan orders.
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The idea: The 500-Year Head Start

Line: Completed 2028. Established 1526.

Every new development in the UAE competes with the same glossy renders. OLIVARA can sell the one thing a render can’t show: time. Each heritage olive arrives with centuries behind it, so a community launching today can look, feel and photograph as established from its first day.

The campaign speaks the developer’s language (handover dates, sales speed, buyer trust) and backs it with the wellbeing case. Shade, cooling and contact with nature make places healthier to live in (Ulrich, 1984), and residents now pay more for them.

Every visual uses my own photography of the trees, so the bark, the scale and the age are real, not rendered.

My own photograph of a heritage olive, with its age set against a handover date. The dates change with each tree’s passport, so the line is always true.

Project 2 — Behaviour change: The Humble Co.

The Other 40% — Brushing does 60%. Finish the job.

Summary

A behaviour change campaign for The Humble Co. that gets young UK adults, especially men, to clean between their teeth every day by showing them the job they leave unfinished.

74% of UK adults don’t floss daily and only 12% follow a dentist-recommended routine. Most people believe brushing is enough. The task: make cleaning between your teeth a daily habit for the groups who skip it most, 18–24s and men.

Research highlights

Market

-   74% Of UK adults don’t floss daily, and only 12% follow a dentist-recommended cleaning routine. (Yorkshire Post, 2026)

-   62% Clean between their teeth regularly. Interdental brushes have overtaken floss, and UK guidance favours them. (Oral Health Foundation, 2017)

-   TePe already owns the topic on TV (“Little Habits That Last a Lifetime”), so a smaller brand needs a sharper, younger voice. (Decision Marketing, n.d.)

Audience

-   18% Of 18–24-year-olds floss daily, compared with 37% of over-55s. (Dental Nursing, n.d.)

-   25% Of men never floss, compared with 16% of women. Around 35% of adults floss daily. (YouGov, 2025)

-   37% Put real effort into their teeth and gums, against healthy eating (52%) and exercise (47%). 32% of Gen Z would improve their routine with more advice. (Yorkshire Post, 2026)

Trend

-   40% Of each tooth’s surface is missed by brushing alone, which cleans only about 60%. (Decision Marketing, n.d.)

-   1 in 4 UK adults have untreated tooth decay, and 9% show signs of severe gum disease. (Yorkshire Post, 2026)

-   The NHS recommends cleaning between teeth daily. A 2016 AP investigation questioned the evidence for floss alone, so this campaign says “clean between your teeth”, not “floss”. (Oral Health Foundation, 2016; HuffPost UK, n.d.)

Insight

“Nobody rinses 60% of a plate and calls it clean, but that is what brushing alone does to your teeth, and three in four of us stop there.”

Strategy: COM-B diagnosis

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Component                           Barrier
  ----------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Capability                          Psychological: people don’t know brushing misses 40%. Physical: floss feels fiddly, and few know interdental brushes exist or which size to use.

  Opportunity                         Physical: the toothbrush sits by the sink, the floss (if bought) is in a drawer. Social: nobody talks about it, so skipping feels normal.

  Motivation (main barrier)           Reflective: “brushing is enough”. Automatic: brushing is a habit; cleaning between isn’t attached to anything.
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Creative brief

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Step                                Answer
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Behaviour                           Clean between your teeth once a day, straight after brushing.

  Audience                            UK adults 18–34 who brush but don’t clean between, with a lean towards men.

  Barrier                             “Brushing is enough”, and no cue for the extra step.

  Trigger                             Salience (show the missing 40%), habit stacking (“After I brush, I clean between”) and ease (interdental brushes kept beside the toothbrush).

  Idea                                The Other 40%: brushing alone leaves the job unfinished.

  Action                              Buy the Finish the Job bundle, scan the QR and start a 30-day streak.

  Measurement                         Interdental sales uplift, QR scans, 30-day streaks completed, pre/post daily-cleaning survey.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The idea: The Other 40%

Line: Brushing misses 40%.

Brushing isn’t wrong, it’s unfinished. The campaign never tells people off. It shows the gap, the other 40% of each tooth, and makes closing it the obvious last step.

The new habit is tied to one people already have. Every execution repeats one plan: “After I brush, I clean between.” Plans in this if-then form make a new behaviour much more likely to happen (Gollwitzer, 1999), and repeating it in the same place makes it automatic over time (Lally et al., 2010).

The Humble Co. is made by dentists, so the message carries professional trust without sounding like a lecture.

Creative outputs

1. 48-sheet & bus shelter

Channel: OOH — near universities and gyms

Copy: Brushing misses 40%. / Clean between your teeth. Every day.

A single tooth split 60/40: the brushed part bright, the rest shaded. One fact and nothing else to read.

Channel: Instagram / TikTok

Copy: You wouldn’t wash 60% of a plate. / So why brush 60% of your teeth?

Everyday jobs left 60% done: a plate, a half-shaved beard, a car washed down one side. Each ends on the tooth. Made for young men who would never accept a half-done job anywhere else.

Channel: Retail pack — pharmacies & online

A toothbrush and interdental brushes sold together, with a mirror cling that states the plan. One pack puts the cue and the tool in the same place.

[TODO] Rewrite in your own words, from your real experience of making the work.

-   I chose The Humble Co. over TePe because TePe has already run a TV campaign on this exact behaviour. A smaller, dentist-made brand needed a sharper idea to be noticed.

Project 3 — FMCG campaign: Altruist

Shirts Off, SPF On. — Made by a skin cancer surgeon. About the price of a pint.

Summary

An FMCG campaign that puts Altruist’s £5.75 dermatologist-made SPF50 in front of British men at the moment the shirt comes off: parks, beer gardens and the BBQ aisle.

Men are 69% more likely to die from melanoma than women, and 41% never use sunscreen in a UK summer. Altruist is made by a skin cancer surgeon and costs about the same as a pint, yet sun care advertising rarely talks to men. The task: grow Altruist in Tesco by making SPF part of a British man’s summer, not just his holiday.

Research highlights

Market

-   250 Tesco stores have stocked Altruist since February 2025. SPF50 100ml costs £5.75, kept low by small margins and little marketing. (Altruist, 2025; Fundraising, n.d.)

-   48% Of UK adults now use SPF daily, up from 22% in 2024, as sun care moves from seasonal to daily. The global category is worth $17.2bn. (Croda Beauty, 2025; Cosmetics Business, 2026)

-   Fewer than 6 in 10 UK adults have bought any sunscreen in the last two years. (Cancer Research UK, 2026b)

Audience

-   41% Of men never use sunscreen in a UK summer (35% of all adults). (Melanoma Focus, 2026)

-   69% More likely to die from melanoma if you are a man. Women’s death rates are falling; men’s are not. (Cancer Research UK, n.d.)

-   2 in 5 Of men’s melanomas are on the torso, possibly linked to going shirtless. (Cancer Research UK, n.d.)

Trend

-   20,000+ UK melanoma cases in 2022, a record, rising to 26,500 a year by 2040. Almost 9 in 10 are caused by UV. (Cancer Research UK, 2026a)

-   36% Of Gen Z apply SPF daily, against 24% of all adults, yet tanning and sunbeds are coming back. (Croda Beauty, 2025)

-   25% Of UK adults have never heard of the UV index. (Melanoma Focus, 2026)

Insight

“Men think sunscreen is for holidays, but their melanomas start at home: two in five are on the torso they bare on the first warm Saturday.”

Strategy: COM-B diagnosis

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Component                           Barrier
  ----------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Capability                          Psychological: they underestimate British sun (a quarter of adults have never heard of the UV index) and don’t link a pink back with cancer.

  Opportunity (main barrier)          Social: SPF reads as a beauty or holiday product, not something mates use at the park. Physical: sunscreen isn’t there when the shirt comes off.

  Motivation                          Reflective: “I don’t burn in England.” Automatic: going shirtless is spontaneous, so nobody plans for it.
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Creative brief

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Step                                Answer
  ----------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Behaviour                           Put on SPF50 before taking the shirt off on warm UK days.

  Audience                            UK men 20–45 who rarely or never use sunscreen at home; Tesco shoppers.

  Barrier                             SPF is seen as a holiday or beauty product, and nobody around them uses it.

  Trigger                             Social norms and timely prompts: put the message, and the product, where the shirt comes off.

  Idea                                Shirts Off, SPF On: a simple rule for the British summer, backed by a skin cancer surgeon.

  Action                              Pick up Altruist SPF50 in the Tesco BBQ aisle with the rest of the weekend shop.

  Measurement                         Sales in BBQ-aisle placements, male buyer share (Clubcard), pre/post “used SPF last weekend” survey, QR scans.
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The idea: Shirts Off, SPF On.

Line: Shirts off? SPF on.

The campaign gives men a rule, not a lecture. “Shirts off, SPF on” is short enough to shout across a park, and it links sunscreen to a moment they already know.

Authority does the persuading. Altruist was founded by consultant dermatologist and skin cancer surgeon Dr Andrew Birnie, who treats skin cancer patients every day. At £5.75, price is no excuse.

Media goes where the behaviour happens (parks, beer gardens, Sunday-league pitches and the Tesco BBQ aisle), so the prompt arrives at the moment of action (Service et al., 2014).

Channel: OOH — parks, beer gardens, seaside routes

Copy: Shirts off? SPF on. / 2 in 5 men’s melanomas are on the torso.

Big, bright and readable from a picnic blanket. The torso fact gives the warning; the price takes away the excuse.

Overall reflection (draft)

At the start of this module I thought health advertising was about telling people the facts. The research for all three campaigns showed me the opposite: people already know they should clean between their teeth or wear sunscreen. The gap is in behaviour, not knowledge, and the COM-B model gave me a way to find the real barrier before writing a single headline.

Each campaign taught me something different. OLIVARA showed me how B2B audiences buy: I had to drop my first idea, “Plant a legacy”, because developers are measured on sales, not legacy. The Humble Co. taught me that accuracy matters; the debate about floss changed my wording to “clean between your teeth”. Altruist taught me to choose an audience with courage, because talking to everyone would have hidden the men who are most at risk.

The tutor update (at least two creative outputs per campaign, with Figma Weave later waived) changed my process in week 8. I moved from planning many small executions to making three strong, connected outputs per campaign.

Next, I would test each idea with real people from the audience before finishing it, for example a short survey of young men about cleaning between their teeth. This links to my next step in healthcare management, where decisions about services should start from evidence about what people actually do.
