1. INTRODUCTION

Two are health-focused and all three are built around prevention, which links to my next step into healthcare management. 2. CAMPAIGN 1 — OLIVARA: "THE 500-YEAR HEAD START" (BRAND CAMPAIGN)

2.1 Challenge
OLIVARA imports mature Spanish heritage olive trees and installs them in the UAE. Its audience is real estate developers, who usually treat trees as a late landscaping cost. The aim is to reposition OLIVARA from supplier to sales asset.

2.2 Research and insight
Dubai recorded 215,700 residential transactions in 2025, up 30.9% (Khaleej Times, 2026a), and 73% of sales were off-plan (Cavendish Maxwell, 2026). Green, wellness-focused projects see up to 40% higher demand (Khaleej Times, 2025b), and competitors such as Aldar and Emaar already build nature-led communities. Developers are paying up to Dh1 million for ancient olive trees (Khaleej Times, 2025a), and olive trees tolerate intense water shortage (Connor, 2005). Green parks can also cool urban areas by up to 2.2°C (Gulf News, 2024), and Dubai plans to plant 3.5 million trees by 2040 (Khaleej Times, 2026b), so mature planting fits the direction of the market.
Insight: off-plan buyers are asked to believe in a place that does not exist yet. A young tree takes years to look established; a 500-year-old olive does it on day one.

2.3 Strategy
The main barrier is reflective motivation: developers see trees as a cost line, not a selling tool. The campaign reframes the tree as proof of quality, with each tree's age and origin documented to remove doubts about survival. Contact with nature also supports wellbeing (Ulrich, 1984), which gives the idea a health benefit. The tone is premium and calm, matching how developers sell their own projects, and the visuals use my own photography so the age of the trees is real rather than rendered.

2.4 Creative idea and outputs
Idea: "The 500-Year Head Start" — give a new community a history before it has residents.
- Trade press spread (Cityscape guide): own photograph of a heritage olive with the line "Completed 2028. Established 1526."
- The Show Tree: a heritage olive installed in the sales gallery at launch, with a plaque reading "This tree is 400 years old. Your home will be ready in 2028."
- The Provenance Pack: direct mail to 50 developers, an olive-wood box with a tree passport (age, grove, origin) and oil from the same grove.

2.5 Measurement
40 qualified enquiries in six months, 20 Cityscape meetings, and 3 of 5 Show Trees converting into masterplan orders.


3. CAMPAIGN 2 — THE HUMBLE CO.: "THE OTHER 40%" (BEHAVIOUR CHANGE)

3.1 Challenge
The behaviour to change is cleaning between your teeth every day. I chose The Humble Co. because it is made by dentists, which adds trust. I avoided TePe because it has already run a TV campaign on this behaviour (Decision Marketing, n.d.). I use the wording "clean between your teeth" because the evidence for floss alone is debated and UK guidance favours interdental brushes (Oral Health Foundation, 2017).

3.2 Research and insight
74% of UK adults do not floss daily and only 12% follow a dentist-recommended routine (Yorkshire Post, 2026). Only 18% of 18–24-year-olds floss daily (Dental Nursing, n.d.), and men skip it more than women (YouGov, 2025). Almost one in four UK adults has untreated tooth decay (Yorkshire Post, 2026), so the habit has a real health cost. Brushing alone cleans about 60% of the tooth surface (Decision Marketing, n.d.).
Insight: nobody would wash 60% of a plate and call it clean, but that is what brushing alone does to your teeth.

3.3 Strategy
Audience: UK adults aged 18–34, with a lean towards men. COM-B shows the main barrier is motivation: people believe brushing is enough, and the new step is not attached to an existing habit. The strategy uses salience (showing the missing 40%), ease (interdental brushes beside the toothbrush) and an if-then plan, "After I brush, I clean between", which makes new behaviour more likely (Gollwitzer, 1999) and automatic over time (Lally et al., 2010). The tone never tells people off; it treats them as capable people who simply have not finished the job.

3.4 Creative idea and outputs
Idea: "The Other 40%" — brushing is not wrong, it is unfinished.
- 48-sheet and bus shelters near universities and gyms: a tooth split 60/40 with the line "Brushing misses 40%."
- Social series for Instagram and TikTok: everyday jobs left 60% done, starting with "You wouldn't wash 60% of a plate."
- "Finish the Job" bundle: a toothbrush and interdental brushes in one pack, with a mirror cling reading "Brush. Then between."

3.5 Measurement
A 20% rise in interdental sales in campaign retailers, a 10-point rise in daily cleaning among 18–34s (pre/post survey), and 25% of QR sign-ups completing a 30-day streak.


4. CAMPAIGN 3 — ALTRUIST: "SHIRTS OFF, SPF ON." (FMCG)

4.1 Challenge
Altruist is an affordable sunscreen founded by consultant dermatologist and skin cancer surgeon Dr Andrew Birnie. SPF50 costs £5.75 in 250 Tesco stores (Altruist, 2025). The aim is to grow sales by making sunscreen part of a British summer, not just a holiday.

4.2 Research and insight
UK melanoma cases passed 20,000 in 2022, and almost 9 in 10 are caused by UV (Cancer Research UK, 2026a). Men are 69% more likely to die from melanoma than women, and two in five men's melanomas are on the torso (Cancer Research UK, n.d.). 41% of men never use sunscreen in a UK summer (Melanoma Focus, 2026), and a quarter of UK adults have never heard of the UV index. At the same time, daily SPF use in the UK rose from 22% to 48% in a year (Croda Beauty, 2025), so the category is growing, but mainly among women and Gen Z.
Insight: men think sunscreen is for holidays, but they burn at home, on the first warm Saturday with their shirt off.

4.3 Strategy
Audience: UK men aged 20–45, because they use sunscreen least and die from melanoma most. The main barrier is social opportunity: SPF is seen as a beauty or holiday product, and it is not there when the shirt comes off. The strategy puts the message and the product at the moment of action (Service et al., 2014), using the founder's medical authority and the low price to remove excuses. Altruist spends little on marketing, so the plan relies on low-cost, high-impact media: the pack, the shelf and a small number of well-placed outdoor sites.

4.4 Creative idea and outputs
Idea: "Shirts Off, SPF On." — a simple rule for the British summer.
- Park and beer-garden 48-sheet: "Shirts off? SPF on." with the fact that 2 in 5 men's melanomas are on the torso.
- "Doctor's Note" limited pack: a prescription-style label reading "Apply before the shirt comes off", signed by Dr Birnie (subject to his approval).
- Tesco BBQ-aisle shelf barker: "Burgers. Buns. SPF50.", placing sunscreen in the weekend shop.

4.5 Measurement
A 25% sales rise in BBQ-aisle stores against control stores, a 10-point rise in male buyers (Clubcard data), and a 15-point rise in men who "used SPF last weekend".


5. REFLECTION

This project showed me that health communication is rarely about missing information. People know they should wear sunscreen or clean between their teeth; the gap is in behaviour. COM-B helped me find the real barrier before writing any headlines.

Each campaign taught me something different. For OLIVARA, I dropped my first line, "Plant a legacy", because it was written for government buyers, while developers are judged on sales. For The Humble Co., the debate about floss changed my wording, and being accurate made the message stronger. For Altruist, choosing men as the audience felt risky, but talking to everyone would have hidden the people most at risk.

The tutor update asking for at least two creative outputs per campaign helped me focus on fewer, stronger pieces. Next time, I would test each idea with the real audience, for example through a short survey, before finishing it. This evidence-first approach is what I want to take into healthcare management.
